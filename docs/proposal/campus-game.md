# A campus that has clearly failed its sanity check

[← Voice-note digest](../brief/voice-notes.md) · [Home](../../README.md) · [Next: layered workflow →](../design/layered-stage-workflow.md)

![Hidden garden and lake reference](../../references/campus/images/hidden-gardens.png)

**Current proposal, 22 October 2026.** A small student explores a familiar campus that behaves increasingly wrong: a pond has another side, a playground hides more than its pipes, and a pocket-sized robot may be the most sensible person here. Funny, atmospheric, a little unsettling. Vatsal remains creative lead; theme, rules and team approval still determine the jam build.

“Poncho Goblins” is the proposed **team name**, not a locked game title or a requirement that the player be a goblin. The earlier [three scope options](game-proposal.md) remain available. This proposal gives the new recordings and local photographs a coherent direction without declaring every idea approved.

## What the player actually does

**Explore → notice a reflection that disagrees → touch the lake → cross a short altered route → return and finish an absurdly ordinary campus task.**

Candidate protagonist: a small freshman in an original oversized hoodie/poncho silhouette. Use a readable shape against large scenery; do not reproduce Six's costume. Walk, jump and one interaction are enough for the first slice. A single mirrored-route trick can provide the Metroidvania-like rediscovery: see an unreachable exit, learn how reflections behave, reach it from the other layer.

### One buildable first slice: the hidden garden / lake

| Beat | Player action | Feedback / proposed joke | Needed content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Garden entrance | Walk through a reed-framed safe route | A tiny “short cut” sign points toward very tall reeds | One entrance screen, basic movement |
| 2. Lake reveal | See a blocked path and an inconsistent reflection | Reflected sign points the useful way | Main lake composition, one visual clue |
| 3. Touch | Interact at a clear marked shore anchor | Brief application-crash-style spin; no copied operating-system window | Reused interaction pose, one transition effect |
| 4. Other side | Traverse a short authored alternate route | An impossible reflection is now an ordinary platform | One alternate collision layout and safe spawn |
| 5. Return / finish | Emerge at the earlier exit, complete the task | “Orientation complete. You are now disoriented.” | Clear goal, ending and restart |

The signs and lines above are **new draft jokes**, not transcribed requirements. One optional robot collectible can beep as if it has issued an important qualification. It can be funny without a shop, pet simulation or dialogue tree.

**Mechanic fallback:** if two collision layouts are unstable, touching the lake triggers the comic transition and moves the student to a paired shore anchor on the same authored route. Preserve the discovery and ending; do not claim a full mirror world was delivered.

## Combat, items and replay: preserve the ideas, price the work

First-run enemy ideas from the audio include drones, campus robots and deliberate digital corruption. One enemy needs idle/patrol, a visible tell, one attack, hit/recovery and reset. If combat is chosen, replace another system rather than appending a combat game to the reflection prototype.

Campus-object weapon seeds: chair swing, cafeteria tray/shield or throw, water bottle, and a progressively smaller computing device. The Leberkäse/defense interpretation is unconfirmed speech recognition. Each weapon needs its own silhouette, reach, animation, collision, feedback and tuning. One chair action is a possible experiment; the full upgrade chain is future scope.

**Replay-only boss ideas:** after completing once, original main encounter slots could receive affectionate professor/campus joke variants; a plush-like Dexter could become humanoid; a yogurt-throwing encounter and Moon Lord/Wall of Flesh comparisons are reference seeds. No portraits or boss likeness art is committed. Main first-run bosses, names, patterns and number are unresolved. A joke skin still needs original art and readable attack tells; it is not a free second campaign.

A private repository or a folder called “our little secret” does **not** hide assets shipped to a browser. Replay gating is a discovery device, not a confidentiality guarantee. Keep unapproved portraits/reference media out of runtime exports.

## Story draft · optional, deliberately small

**Working premise: “Introduction to Getting Lost.”** A new student is sent to collect a campus orientation stamp. The map is perfectly clear; reality is less cooperative. The student follows a misplaced reflection through the hidden garden and returns to a place they can finally recognize. A found robot gives a very official beep. The final instruction is simply to help the next lost student.

The warmth comes from finding your footing somewhere new, not a long explanation of the corruption. Environmental jokes introduce uncertainty without mocking a real individual. On a later replay, the same places could reveal a different explanation and optional comic boss replacements. Infinity Blade contributes a different worldbuilding idea: a civilization builds around enormous advanced remnants it only partly understands, presenting them as ancient machinery, ritual or magic. An optional original seed is a lake mechanism treated as a ceremonial gate that responds to an ordinary student ID. This does not decide the final lore, add a new mechanic or make replay the purpose of the reference.

This is a disposable writing draft for discussion, not the requested final story direction. Alexander may supply that later, and Vatsal can reject this premise entirely.

## Stop expanding when these are true

A fresh player understands the goal, reaches the lake, recognizes the interaction, crosses/returns safely, finds the ending and restarts. Jump and collision feel consistent on the soft-looking ground. The route works without following development instructions. The exported browser game contains original approved assets and credits, not campus portraits or commercial screenshots.

**Jam cut order:** replay bosses → extra locations → weapon chain → companion behavior → combat → second physical world. Keep one readable route, one enjoyable reveal, safe retry and an ending. The [jam plan](../delivery/jam-plan.md) uses the published 18 sprint hours rather than assuming 24 productive hours.

## Design interpretation and dependencies

The recognizable loop is enter an encounter, read threats, dodge/fire, receive a reward, then try another room. Humor can sit in improbable weapons and tiny reactions rather than long dialogue. An exaggerated premise does not prevent meaningful stakes, but the specific comic voice must be original.

Projectile combat requires spawn/collision/despawn rules, clear telegraphing, aim controls and enemy behavior. Random rooms require navigable connections and fair spawn placement. Many weapons multiply balance and asset work; co-op introduces synchronization and failure cases. Importing all of these into a platformer is a substantial redesign, not a skin change.

For our own prototype, one readable enemy with one projectile pattern could replace a static hazard **only if combat is selected** and the full loop already works. Keep a single attack/weapon. No loot rarity, random floor generator, roster, co-op or metaprogression in the baseline. A deliberately absurd prop or enemy reaction can supply humor without those systems.
